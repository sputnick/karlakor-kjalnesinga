# Óðurinn til gleðinnar

Fagra gleði, guða logi,  
Gimlis dóttir, heill sé þér!  
Í þinn hásal hrifnir eldi,  
heilög gyðja, komum vér.  
Þínir blíðu töfrar tengja,  
tískan meðan sundur slær;  
allir bræður aftur verða  
yndisvængjum þínum nær.  

Hver þann dýrgrip hefir hlotið  
hjartans vin að eiga sér,  
hver, sem festi fríðan svanna  
fagran, syngi nú sem vér,  
já, hver aðeins eina sálu  
eignar sér um víða storð,  
hinn, sem enga á, skal kveðja  
angurs tárum þetta borð!  

Gleði fyrir allt, sem andar  
allífs brjóstið, móðir vor:  
Allir góðir, allir vondir  
elta gleði blómaspor.  
Oss hún veitir vín og kossa,  
vinar faðm, sem tryggð ei brá,  
munað gaf hún minnsta ormi,  
mesta seraf, Guð að sjá.  

## Notes

- **Stanzas:** 3 set in the score, 3 written here.
- **Form:** Matthías Jochumsson's Icelandic translation of Schiller's 'An die Freude' (Ode to Joy), set to Beethoven's melody from the 9th Symphony; the 3 stanzas transcribed correspond one-to-one to Schiller's stanzas 1-3 ('Freude, schöner Götterfunken' / 'Wem der große Wurf gelungen' / 'Freude trinken alle Wesen'), which independently confirms the reconstruction. The score also prints an alternate final line for stanza 1, '2. allar systur aftur verða yndisvængjum þínum nær' (systur = sisters, instead of bræður = brothers), evidently an alternate lyric for a mixed-voice edition; this TTBB score sings 'allir bræður', so the alternate is omitted from the lyrics and noted here instead. In stanza 3 (bars 38-58) the Bassi enters two bars behind the Tenor in a short canon on the same words, then both parts restate the closing couplet together in the final ritardando; only one pass of the text is written here per the no-duplication rule.
- Stanza 1 confirmed word-for-word against Matthías Jochumsson's translation 'Til gleðinnar' on Icelandic Wikisource (is.wikisource.org, pageid 1769), which only carries this first stanza. That source has 'guða' lowercase (genitive plural of 'guð', not a proper noun) and hyphenates 'yndis-vængjum'; lowercased 'guða' here accordingly and kept 'yndisvængjum' as one word (same word, immaterial hyphenation choice). Stanzas 2-3 were not found online (web search tools were unavailable for this batch); they are transcribed from the score's very clean embedded text layer and independently cross-validated by their one-to-one correspondence to Schiller's original German stanzas 2-3 ('Wem der große Wurf gelungen' / 'Freude trinken alle Wesen'), giving high confidence. Kept 'seraf' (not 'serafi') in the last stanza's closing line ('mesta seraf, Guð að sjá') as printed — the shorter, undeclined form fits the melody's fixed syllable count exactly (matching 'und der Cherub steht vor Gott') and is consistent with 19th-century translation style.
