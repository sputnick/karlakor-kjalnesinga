# Gullvagninn

Sendu nú vagninn þinn að sækja mig,  
sendu nú gullvagninn að sækja mig.  
Gættu mín, geymdu mig, gefðu mér friðinn,  
langt hef ég farið og mig langar heim.  

Lengi hef ég reikað þennan refilstig,  
rökkvar senn og þreytan er að buga mig.  
Hvar fæ ég að halla mínu höfði nú?  
Herra, enginn getur bjargað nema þú.  

(Viðlag)  

## Notes

- **Stanzas:** 2 set in the score, 2 written here.
- **Form:** TTBB octavo, úts. Þórir Baldursson. The opening verse/refrain is sung three times (start, middle, end) with identical words, marked '(Viðlag)' at its last recurrence. Between the 1st and 3rd statements the choir sings an extended 'já/hm' vocalise; hidden inside it, the baritone part alone carries the second real verse ('Lengi hef ég reikað...'), while the bass part simultaneously sings a different, shorter variant of its first two lines ('Líður þessi dagur senn og dimma fer, djúpir eru skuggarnir sem þrengja að mér') before both parts converge on 'Hvar fæ ég að halla mínu höfði nú? Herra, enginn getur bjargað nema þú.' The scat syllables 'Babb babb babb bara' and all 'já/hm/ó/úó' vocalise are omitted as filler.
- Icelandic-language cover ('Sendu nú vagninn þinn að sækja mig') of an American spiritual/gospel song ('Swing Down Chariot', per the score's own subtitle), arranged by Þórir Baldursson. The bass part's alternate first couplet during the bridge ('Líður þessi dagur senn og dimma fer, djúpir eru skuggarnir sem þrengja að mér') is a genuine simultaneous alternate text sung by only one voice part; it is described here rather than inserted into the main lyric to avoid presenting two different simultaneous texts as sequential. Upgrade pass: found the full published text on guitarparty.com, credited to lyricist Jónas Friðrik Guðnason (performed by Björgvin Halldórsson) - it matches the score's top/melody line exactly for the refrain (the score's alto and bass parts alone carry extra echo words 'Ég bið þig' and 'já, herra' at the very start, which is why the main melody line transcribed here doesn't have them) and matches the verse wording, confirming the transcription. One word differs: guitarparty.com has 'reykað' ('smoked') where the score clearly and legibly prints 'reikað' ('wandered', matching the idiom 'reika refilstigu' - to wander astray - and the line's sense); kept the score's 'reikað' as the more sensible reading of a folk lyric probably mistyped on the chord site.
