# Abendständchen

Schlafe, Liebchen, weil's auf Erden  
nun so still und einsam wird!  
Oben gehn die goldnen Heerden,  
für uns alle wacht der Hirt.  

Schlingend sich an Bäum' und Zweigen,  
in dein stilles Kämmerlein  
wie auf goldnen Leitern steigen  
diese Töne aus und ein.  

Und der Töne Klang entführet  
weit der buhlerische Wind,  
und durch Schloß und Wand ihn spüret  
träumend wohl das süße Kind.  

## Notes

- **Stanzas:** 3 set in the score, 3 written here.
- **Form:** No. 2 ('Abendständchen', composed 1839) of Mendelssohn's 'Vier Lieder für vierstimmigen Männerchor', Op. 75 (Breitkopf & Härtel edition, M.B. 131). Three verses are printed stacked under one 8-bar strophic melody, each ending in an overlapping echo between the two tenor and two bass parts (e.g. verse 1's 'für uns wacht der Hirt' is passed between voices as 'Alle wacht der Hirt ... für uns Alle wacht der Hirt ... für uns Alle wacht der Hirt'); these echoes are the same words repeated between voice parts for canon-like effect, so each stanza's text was written once.
- Found and confirmed via Firecrawl search after the session's WebSearch budget ran out: the choral songbook 'In Freud und Leid' prints this exact 3-verse Eichendorff text (titled 'Ständchen' there, as in Eichendorff's own 'Aus dem Leben eines Taugenichts', where the 1-stanza version confirms 'einsam' as the score's wording, not the variant 'seltsam' found in some other prints) with 'für uns alle wacht der Hirt' - this corrects an earlier score-transcribed pass that dropped the word 'alle' from that line. This score PDF ('Abendständchen page2.pdf') is actually the full 4-song collection (the other PDF, 'Abendstandchen.pdf', is a duplicate scan of just this song's own page). The other three songs in the collection - 'Der frohe Wandersmann' (No. 1, Eichendorff), 'Trinklied' (No. 3, Goethe) and 'Abschiedstafel' (No. 4, Eichendorff) - are listed in extra_songs and were not independently checked online; none of them appear elsewhere in all-titles.json. The choir's own Vor 2017 concert programme lists this song's credit as 'Höf: Felix Mendelssohn. Texti: Bartholdy' (i.e. crediting the composer's own surname as a separate lyricist), which is clearly a data-entry error in that programme, not used here.
