# Jólasnjór

Jólasnjórinn svífur niður,  
svífur ofan úr skýjunum niður.  
Jólasnjór skínandi umvefur allt,  
tindrandi, glitrandi himnanna skart.  

Jólasnjórinn svífur niður yfir stræti og torg,  
svífur ofan úr skýjunum niður.  
Nú er allt sem leyst úr læðing, enginn leiði né sorg,  
nú er lífsgleðin ríkjandi í borg.  

Jólasnjór, jólasnjór, jólasnjór, jólasnjór,  
skínandi umvefur allt.  
Sindrandi, sindrandi, tindrandi, tindrandi,  
glitrandi himnanna skart.  

Ljósin ljóma, bjöllur hljóma, óma hlátrar og sköll,  
allir brosa og heilsast með gleði.  
Ys og læti, líf og kæti, ljómar veröldin öll,  
jólagleði í hreysi og höll.  

(Viðlag)  

## Notes

- **Stanzas:** 4 set in the score, 5 written here.
- **Form:** Score: tenor solo intro (mm.1-16, bass humming 'Ao') states a shortened preview of the refrain; A tempo section (mm.17-47) then presents verse 1 and the refrain, repeated a second time with verse 2's words under the same music, ending with a short hummed coda. Verse 2's text is engraved as a simultaneous second underlay at the same bars as verse 1/refrain rather than written out separately afterward; it is given here as its own stanza in singing order. The final repeat of the refrain (2nd ending, 'Ljósin... skart... mm') is marked (Viðlag) rather than repeated in full.
- Verified verse and refrain wording against the KVAK (Kvennakór Akureyrar) song page, which credits the same lyricist/composer and gives matching text (confirmed 'sköll' and 'ljómar veröldin öll', correcting the score-adjacent OCR's 'hljómar'). Icelandic Wikipedia's track listing for the 1971 Vilhjálmur og Ellý Vilhjálms LP confirms the lyricist spelling 'Jóhanna G. Erlingsson' (score also uses this; a Kvak.is meta tag has 'Jóhann', taken to be their typo). Composer given on the score as 'Jay Livingston and Rey Evans'; 'Rey' is almost certainly a scan/typo of 'Ray'. The tenor-solo introductory line (mm.1-16) is not part of the commercially published song text and was transcribed directly from the score's underlay as it carries real sung words, not vocalise.
